What the blocks measured

Every actuator group was randomised over two-hour blocks, so the campaign itself answers the question “what if this group had been at full rather than off?”. Here is that measured answer next to each twin’s, with every block simulated by a model that never saw its day.

How to read it. Each row is one actuator group, and every value is the change in the block-mean temperature (or humidity) from switching that group from off to full for the 2 h, with everything else as it was. Left of the zero line means it cools (or dries); right means it warms (or humidifies). The dot is the measured answer, estimated from the randomised blocks with the weather taken into account; its whisker is ± one standard error. The blue bar and the orange bar are what the 15-min and 1-h twins predict for the same switch, averaged over the blocks. A good twin puts its bar near the dot. A bar on the other side of zero from the dot means the twin has the sign wrong. For example, the 1-h twin puts the roof vents slightly on the warming side, where the blocks measured about 1.3 °C of cooling.

The twins on this page are fitted on all of August so that any block can be simulated in the browser; the panel above uses the paper’s out-of-sample results (cross-validation by day).
